# Information Security Policy

Fieldwork Studio Ltd — sample corpus for the DocChat demo. Fictional company; not legal advice.

Version 2.0, approved by the directors on 8 January 2024 and reviewed every twelve months. This policy applies to all staff and subcontractors, and to every system that stores Studio or client information.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy sets the minimum security controls the Studio applies to its own systems and to client systems it can access. Where an SOW or a client's own policy sets stricter requirements, the stricter requirement applies for that client. Data protection obligations for personal data are set out in the Privacy Notice and the Data Processing Addendum.

## 2. Roles

### 2.1 Security lead

One director acts as security lead. The security lead owns this policy, approves exceptions, runs the annual review, and is the first contact for any suspected incident.

### 2.2 Everyone

Everyone who works for the Studio must complete security training when they join and every year after, report suspected incidents immediately, and follow this policy. Breaches of this policy may lead to disciplinary action or the end of a subcontract.

## 3. Access control

### 3.1 Accounts

Every person has their own named account on every system; shared logins are not allowed, except for client-provided service accounts stored in the password manager. Multi-factor authentication is required on email, file storage, source control, the password manager and any client system that supports it.

### 3.2 Least privilege

People are given access only to the clients and systems they are working on. Production access to client systems is limited to the project lead and the developers who need it, and is reviewed at the end of each Project.

### 3.3 Leavers

When someone leaves the Studio or a subcontract ends, their access to all Studio and client systems is removed on their last working day, and any shared credentials they knew are rotated within five Business Days.

## 4. Devices

Laptops must have full-disk encryption, automatic screen lock after five minutes, a supported operating system with automatic security updates, and endpoint protection. Client data must not be stored on personal phones or on removable media. A lost or stolen device must be reported to the security lead within one hour of discovery so that it can be wiped remotely.

## 5. Passwords and secrets

### 5.1 Password manager

All credentials are kept in the Studio password manager. Passwords must be generated by the password manager and be unique to each system.

### 5.2 Secrets in code

API keys, database passwords and other secrets must never be committed to source control. They are stored in the hosting provider's environment settings or a secrets manager. If a secret is committed by mistake, it is treated as compromised and rotated the same day, even if the commit is removed.

## 6. Development practices

### 6.1 Code review

Every change to a client's production code is reviewed by a second developer before it is deployed, except for urgent fixes under the Retainer and Support Terms, which are reviewed within one Business Day after deployment.

### 6.2 Dependencies

Dependencies are kept up to date. Critical security updates to dependencies in production are applied within seven days of release for clients with a Retainer or managed hosting.

### 6.3 Testing and data

Test and staging environments must not contain real personal data unless the client has approved it in writing. Where real data is needed, it is minimised and deleted when testing ends.

## 7. Backups

For managed hosting, databases are backed up daily and kept for 30 days, with at least one copy stored in a separate region. Restores are tested every quarter. Clients who manage their own hosting are responsible for their own backups.

## 8. Incident response

### 8.1 Reporting

Anyone who suspects a security incident, such as a compromised account, a lost device, malware or data sent to the wrong person, must tell the security lead immediately by phone and follow up in writing.

### 8.2 Handling

The security lead contains the incident, preserves evidence, and decides whether affected clients must be told. Where client personal data is affected, the client is notified without undue delay and within 48 hours of discovery, in line with the Data Processing Addendum and section 9 of the Privacy Notice.

### 8.3 Review

Every incident is written up within ten Business Days, with its cause and the changes made to prevent it happening again.

## 9. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy must be approved in writing by the security lead, be time-limited, and be recorded with the reason and any extra controls.
